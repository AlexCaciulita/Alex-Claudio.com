03 / The timing

A wedding timeline
with time for your guests.

Plan portraits around the ceremony, daylight, and the time you want to spend with your guests.

By Alex Claudio Photography
September 16, 2026

Decide how much of the reception you want to spend with your guests before you fill the afternoon with portraits.

If you want to be at cocktail hour, put it on the schedule as time for you, not as a gap. If you want to see each other for the first time at the ceremony, reserve time for portraits together afterward.

We can plan the photographs with you. Your planner or coordinator fits them alongside transport, catering, and the ceremony, then shares one agreed schedule with everyone.

Make the decisions before adding times

Start with what cannot move

List the fixed points first: venue access, the ceremony time and length, meal service, transport departures, and the end of photography coverage. Add religious or cultural traditions, outfit changes, and family commitments.

Check daylight for your date and location using the U.S. Naval Observatory’s sun and twilight times, with the correct daylight-saving setting. In Seattle, a June sunset comes after 9 p.m. and a December one before 4:30 p.m., so a timeline borrowed from another season rarely fits.

Sunset time alone will not tell you when direct light leaves a courtyard behind a tall building. Visit the venue near your ceremony time if you can, and ask us how trees, walls, indoor light, and the forecast affect your portrait options.

Choose when to see each other

A first look means seeing each other before the ceremony. You can then take some or all portraits together, with the wedding party, and with family earlier, depending on who is ready and available.

It also means finishing preparations earlier. Check with the people doing your hair and makeup, and give everyone needed for group photographs their arrival time.

If you prefer to wait until the ceremony, you can still take individual portraits and some family photographs beforehand. Reserve time afterward for the pictures that need both of you, and decide how much of cocktail hour you are willing to miss.

Write the family list with names

Write every photograph as a list of names. “Both of us with our parents” leaves questions when families are blended or relationships are complicated. “Us with Maria and David, then us with Sam and Priya” does not.

Tell us privately about sensitivities, mobility needs, or combinations you want to avoid. Nobody should have to explain a difficult family situation in front of a waiting group.

Ask someone who knows the family to gather the next group while we photograph the current one. Give everyone on the list a time and meeting place before the wedding, and we will estimate how long the finished list needs.

Part 2 of 2

Build a schedule you can follow

An example with a 4 p.m. ceremony

This eight-hour example runs from 1–9 p.m. It assumes one venue, a first look, a short family list, a 30-minute ceremony, good afternoon light, and no off-site travel. Hair and makeup finish in time for dressing and portraits.

Time Plan
1:00-1:45 p.m. Final preparations, getting dressed, and personal details.
1:45-2:00 p.m. Readiness check and move to the first-look location.
2:00-2:35 p.m. First look and portraits together.
2:35-3:00 p.m. Wedding-party portraits, if you have a wedding party.
3:00-3:25 p.m. Family portraits from the agreed list.
3:25-4:00 p.m. A break for you, while we photograph the ceremony setup and arriving guests.
4:00-4:30 p.m. Ceremony.
4:30-5:20 p.m. A few private minutes together, then cocktail hour with your guests.
5:20-5:45 p.m. Guests seated, your entrance, and the welcome.
5:45-7:00 p.m. Dinner; coordinate vendor meals and any planned events with catering.
7:00-7:30 p.m. Toasts or other planned reception events.
7:30-8:00 p.m. First dance and any other planned dances.
8:00-9:00 p.m. Open dancing and candid reception photographs; coverage ends at 9 p.m.

Because the group portraits are finished before the ceremony, you get most of cocktail hour with your guests. The 25 minutes for family photographs is a placeholder; use the estimate from your own list.

Coverage ends at 9 p.m., so a later exit needs extra time. For evening portraits, pick a time based on the light at your venue and check it with your planner and catering team.

Without a first look, rebuild the portrait schedule after the ceremony. Work out which family groups can happen beforehand and how long the rest will take, then check the total before trying to fit it all into cocktail hour.

Count the time between events

Getting from a suite to a courtyard includes collecting belongings, waiting for the lift, and making sure everyone arrives. A driving estimate leaves out boarding the shuttle, parking, and walking into the next venue.

Put those transitions on the schedule. Leave time to eat, drink water, use the bathroom, and settle in before the ceremony. Agree in advance which optional photographs can move or shrink if something runs late.

Before sharing the final version, check the ceremony length, family list, transport, meals, and photography finish time with the people responsible for each.

Send us your date, venue, and draft plans, and we will help you fit portraits and coverage into the day.
