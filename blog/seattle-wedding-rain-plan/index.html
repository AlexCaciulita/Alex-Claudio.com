01 / The setting

A Seattle wedding
rain plan.

Choose an indoor space you like, agree on when to make the weather call, and plan the walks between rooms.

By Alex Claudio Photography
September 16, 2026

Before you spend time choosing umbrellas, choose where you would be happy to get married if it rains.

Then plan how everyone moves between that room, your portrait spaces, and dinner. When the wet-weather version of the day works on paper, you can stop rebuilding the schedule every time the forecast changes, and a dry afternoon simply gives you more places to go.

Part 1 of 2

Choose your spaces

Walk the indoor ceremony setup

Ask the venue to show you the backup arrangement during your visit, or a photograph of it set for a wedding your size. An empty room can look generous until you add chairs, an aisle, musicians, and flowers.

Stand where you would exchange vows and look for the windows. Light from the side or in front of you falls evenly across your faces. A bright window directly behind you turns you both into silhouettes, so ask whether the ceremony can face another way.

Before you sign off on the room, get answers to these:

  • Can it hold the full guest list comfortably, with an aisle wide enough to walk?
  • Which flowers and decorations can move inside, and who moves them?
  • What rental or setup costs would be added?
  • If the room changes from ceremony to dinner, where do guests wait during the change?
  • Can guests who need step-free access reach every space?

Ask your photographer to see the room too, in person or from your photographs. A corner that works for a portrait of the two of you may be too tight for a family group of twenty.

Find two covered portrait spots

Choose one space for portraits of the two of you and a larger one for family photographs. Covered porches, deep doorways, arcades, and rooms with tall windows all work well. Overcast light is soft, and standing just inside a doorway that faces open sky gives you even, flattering light on the greyest afternoon.

Check the background, how you get there, and whether staff will be setting up nearby during your portrait time. A covered entrance may also be the delivery route. A hotel lobby may welcome visitors but require permission for a photography session. Confirm access before either goes on the schedule.

For public locations, check the Seattle Parks photography permit requirements for your location and intended use. An approved space at your venue can spare you a separate drive in the rain.

Part 2 of 2

Agree on the day’s decisions

Split the weather call in two

Your venue may need an early answer so staff can arrange seating and rentals. Ask for its exact deadline and write it down, along with the name of the person who makes the ceremony call.

Portraits can usually be decided much later, sometimes an hour before. Tell your photographer ahead of time whether you would enjoy a short walk in light rain or prefer to stay dry, so the choice on the day is quick.

Ask your planner or venue coordinator to pass each decision to the florist, musicians, transport team, and photographers. You should not have to relay the same message while you are getting ready.

Read the forecast by the hour

A 40% chance of rain does not mean it will rain for 40% of the day. The National Weather Service defines it as the chance of measurable precipitation at a particular point during a specified period.

The hourly forecast for your venue tells you more. Look at the timing, expected rainfall, and wind across your wedding hours. A dry half hour at 5 p.m. may be all your portraits need. Review those details with your team at the agreed check-in, keeping any earlier venue deadline in mind.

Plan the short walks

The route from the suite to the ceremony still counts, even if it takes two minutes. Keep suitable shoes, a towel, an extra layer, and touch-up supplies in one bag, and decide who carries it.

Confirm who brings umbrellas and where they will be kept. Clear umbrellas let light reach your faces in photographs; dark ones throw a shadow and hide the dress.

Arrange help with a train or veil if you need it. Tell your team what must stay dry, and check where guests can leave wet coats without blocking an entrance. After outdoor portraits, leave ten minutes to dry off and settle before the next part of the day.

Send one page to your team

A week before the wedding, send everyone involved a single page with:

  • The ceremony decision deadline and who makes the call
  • The confirmed portrait spaces, indoors and out
  • Guest routes and accessibility needs
  • Who brings the umbrellas, towels, and the bag
  • Your preference about portraits in light rain

Send us your date and venue, and we can talk through where photography fits in both versions of your day.
