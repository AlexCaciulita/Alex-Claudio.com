05 / The session

An engagement session
in Seattle.

Where to go in each season, when to start, what to wear, and why it helps to do it a few months before the wedding.

By Alex Claudio Photography
October 31, 2026

An engagement session is about an hour with us, somewhere you like, a few months before the wedding.

You come away with photographs for your save-the-dates and your walls, and by the wedding day you already know how we work. Here is how we plan one around Seattle, from when to book it to what to bring.

Part 1 of 2

Planning the session

Book it around the save-the-dates

If you want engagement photographs on your save-the-dates, count back from when the cards go out. Most couples send them six to eight months before the wedding. For a wedding next September, that means cards in the mail by February and a session before the end of this year, with time left for editing and printing.

If the photographs are only for you, any season works. Some couples choose a different season from their wedding so the two galleries look different: spring blossom for an autumn wedding, winter coats for a summer one.

Choose a place you already love

The best places are usually ones you already know, like the beach where you walk on Sunday mornings or the trail where you got engaged. You relax faster somewhere familiar, and years from now you will recognize the place as well as the two of you.

If nowhere comes to mind, tell us whether you are drawn to water, trees, or the city, and we will suggest two or three places that suit the season. One location fills the hour. A second one means a short drive and about half an hour more.

Match the place to the season

Most of our favorite places around Seattle look their best for only a few months of the year.

Season Where to go
Spring, March to May Azalea Way in the Washington Park Arboretum, in bloom in April and May. The Skagit Valley tulip fields in April, about an hour north; go on a weekday.
Summer, June to August Golden Gardens, with the sun going down over Puget Sound. The bluffs and beach at Discovery Park. Paradise at Mount Rainier, when the wildflowers bloom from late July.
Autumn, September to November The Arboretum and Volunteer Park once the maples turn in October. Gas Works Park, with the skyline across Lake Union.
Winter, December to February Pike Place Market and Post Alley, where there is cover and plenty of light. Kerry Park after dark, with the whole city below you.

Paradise is about two and a half hours each way, so plan on a full day. The tulip farms and Mount Rainier charge for entry, and some gardens want photographers to have a permit. We check the rules before we settle on a place.

Start ninety minutes before sunset

Our favorite light is the last hour before sunset, when the sun is low and warm and lights your faces from the side. If you start about ninety minutes before sunset, the second half of your session falls in that hour.

In Seattle that start time moves a long way through the year. In late June the sun sets at 9:10 p.m. and you would start around 7:40. In mid-December it sets at 4:18 p.m., which means starting before 3. The good part of a winter session is that the city lights are on by 5, so you can finish at Kerry Park with the skyline lit and still be home for dinner.

On gray days, and a Seattle winter has plenty of them, the light stays soft all afternoon and the start time matters less.

If it rains

Light rain is fine, and we often like it. Wet pavement shines, and you have an excuse to stand close under one umbrella. Bring a clear one if you have it, because a black umbrella puts both your faces in shadow.

For heavier rain, keep a covered place near your main location: the arcades at Pike Place Market, a café you like, or your own living room. If the forecast looks bad the day before, we decide together whether to go ahead, move under cover, or pick another date. Our wedding rain plan explains how to read the hourly forecast.

Part 2 of 2

On the day

Dress for the place

Wear clothes you would choose for a good dinner out, in colors that go together without matching. Solid colors and soft patterns photograph best. Fine stripes and small checks can shimmer on a screen, and big logos pull the eye away from your faces.

Then think about the ground. Heels sink into grass and sand, so for a beach or a meadow wear shoes you can walk in and carry the nice pair. From November to March your coat will be in most of the photographs, so pick it as carefully as what you wear under it.

One change of clothes is enough

A second outfit gets you two looks from one session, say something dressed up for the city and something relaxed for the beach. Each change takes ten to fifteen minutes and you need somewhere private, usually the back seat of a car. A third outfit starts to eat into the light.

Bring a small bag with water, lip balm, a hair tie, and a comb, and leave it with us while you walk.

Bring the dog, and a friend

If you have a dog, bring it. For the first part of the session you are both watching the dog instead of the camera, and those photographs are often the most relaxed of the day.

Ask a friend to come along to hold the leash between photographs and take the dog home after twenty minutes. A dog waiting in a parked car or tied to a bench keeps half your attention, and for the rest of the session we want your hands free for each other.

Give it ten minutes

Most couples feel stiff for the first ten minutes, and we plan for it. We start with a walk. You talk to each other while we hang back, so your hands and feet have something to do before anyone asks you to stand still.

You do not need to know how to pose. We tell you where to stand and which way to face, then give you something to do there, like whispering the worst joke you know or walking ahead and looking back, and we photograph whatever happens.

Treat it as a rehearsal

By the end of the session you know how close we stand and what we mean by “walk toward us,” and you have mostly stopped noticing the camera. We know what makes you laugh. On the wedding day, portraits may get twenty minutes between the ceremony and dinner, and you do not spend any of it getting used to us.

The photographs are handy after the save-the-dates too, on a welcome sign at the reception or as a framed print for your parents.

You can add an engagement session to any of our wedding collections, and the largest one includes it. Tell us your date and a place you love, and we will plan it with you.
