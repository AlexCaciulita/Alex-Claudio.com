05 / The session

An engagement session
in Seattle.

Where to go in each season, when to start, what to wear, and why it helps to do it a few months before the wedding.

By Alex Claudio Photography
October 31, 2026

An engagement session is an hour or two with us, somewhere you like, a few months before the wedding.

You come away with photographs for your save-the-dates and your walls, and you learn how we work before the wedding day. These are the decisions that shape a session in and around Seattle, from when to book it to what to bring.

Planning the session

Book it around the save-the-dates

If you want engagement photographs on your save-the-dates, count back from when the cards go out. Most couples send them six to eight months before the wedding. For a wedding next September, that means cards in the mail by February and a session before the end of this year, with time left for editing and printing.

If the photographs are only for you, any season works. Some couples choose a different season from their wedding so the two galleries look different: spring blossom for an autumn wedding, winter coats for a summer one.

Choose a place you already love

The best locations are usually ones you know: the beach where you walk on Sunday mornings, the street with your favorite bakery, the trail where one of you proposed. You relax faster somewhere familiar, and years later the photographs show a place that meant something to you.

If nowhere comes to mind, choose by setting: water, trees, or city. Tell us which, and we will suggest two or three places that suit the season. One location fills an hour comfortably. A second one means a short drive and about half an hour more.

Match the place to the season

Most of the places we like around Seattle are at their best for a few months of the year. Here are some of them, season by season.

Season Where to go
Spring, March to May Azalea Way in the Washington Park Arboretum, in bloom in April and May. The Skagit Valley tulip fields in April, about an hour north; go on a weekday.
Summer, June to August Golden Gardens, with the sun going down over Puget Sound. The bluffs and beach at Discovery Park. Paradise at Mount Rainier, when the wildflowers bloom from late July.
Autumn, September to November The Arboretum and Volunteer Park once the maples turn in October. Gas Works Park, with the skyline across Lake Union.
Winter, December to February Pike Place Market and Post Alley, covered and lit. Kerry Park after dark, with the city lit up below you.

Paradise is about two and a half hours each way, so it turns the session into a day trip. The tulip farms and Mount Rainier charge for entry, and some gardens ask photographers for a permit, so we check the rules before we settle on a place.

Start ninety minutes before sunset

The softest light of the day comes in the last hour before sunset, when the sun is low and warm and lights your faces from the side. Start about ninety minutes before sunset and the second half of your session falls in that hour.

In Seattle that start time moves a long way through the year. In late June the sun sets at 9:10 p.m., so you would start around 7:40. In mid-December it sets at 4:18 p.m., so you would start before 3. The city lights are on by 5, so a winter session can end at Kerry Park with the skyline lit, and you are still home for dinner.

On the overcast days Seattle gets through most of the winter, the light is soft all afternoon and the exact time matters less.

Plan for rain, lightly

Light rain photographs well. Wet pavement shines, colors deepen, and you have a reason to stand close under one umbrella. Bring a clear umbrella if you can. It lets the light reach your faces, where a black one throws a shadow.

For heavier rain, keep a covered place near your main location: the arcades at Pike Place Market, a café you like, or your own home. If the forecast looks bad the day before, we decide together whether to go ahead, move under cover, or choose another date. Our wedding rain plan explains how to read the hourly forecast.

On the day

Dress for the place

Wear clothes you would choose for a good dinner out, in colors that sit well together without matching. Solid colors and soft patterns photograph best. Fine stripes and small checks can shimmer on a screen, and large logos pull the eye away from your faces.

Then think about the ground. Heels sink into grass and sand, so for a beach or a meadow wear shoes you can walk in and bring the nicer pair in a bag. From November to March your coat will be in most of the photographs, so choose it with as much care as what is underneath.

Bring one change of clothes, not three

A second outfit gives you two looks from one session, something dressed up for the city and something relaxed for the beach. Each change takes ten to fifteen minutes and needs somewhere private, usually the back seat of a car. More than two outfits starts to eat into the light.

Bring a small bag with water, lip balm, a hair tie, and a comb, and leave it with us while you walk.

Bring the dog, and a friend

If you have a dog, bring it. In the first part of a session you are both watching the dog instead of the camera, and those photographs are often the most relaxed of the day.

Ask a friend to come along, hold the leash between photographs, and take the dog home after twenty minutes. A dog waiting in a parked car or tied to a bench keeps half your attention, and the rest of the session needs your hands free for each other.

Give it ten minutes

Most couples feel stiff for the first ten minutes, and we plan for it. We start with a walk: you talk to each other while we stay back, so your hands and feet have something to do before anyone asks you to stand still.

You do not need to know how to pose. We tell you where to stand and which way to face, then give you something to do there: whisper the worst joke you know, tell each other about your first date, walk ahead and look back. We photograph what happens next.

Treat it as a rehearsal

By the end of the session you will know how we work: how close we stand, what we mean by “walk toward us,” and that you can stop thinking about the camera. We will know what makes you laugh and how you stand together. On the wedding day, when portraits may have twenty minutes between the ceremony and dinner, none of that time goes on getting used to each other.

The photographs also work beyond the save-the-dates: a welcome sign at the reception, a photo guest book, a framed print for each set of parents.

You can add an engagement session to your wedding with us. Tell us your date and the places you love, and we will plan the session with you.
